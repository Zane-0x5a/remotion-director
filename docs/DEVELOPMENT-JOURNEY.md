# Development journey

The goal never changed: make a model design work that earns a second look, on its own, judged on its real pixels. What changed was the belief about *how*. This is the story of the two architectures that chased that goal and died, why each died, how the third was shaped by their post-mortems, how the kit around the third was rebuilt when the model generation changed, and where human judgment did the load-bearing work: in the *building* of the pipeline, not in the designing the pipeline itself does. (Those two layers are distinct throughout. The product designs on its own; the human's contribution was the method and discipline that got it there. More on that at the end.)

> A note on how this was built: AI did much of the coding and drafting throughout — that is part of the method, not something to hide. What makes it the opposite of vibe-coding is that the human stayed decisively in the driver's seat. Every architectural turn below was forced by an observed phenomenon in a controlled experiment, and the direction was set by human judgment and experiment, never left to drift on the model's defaults. The arc reads as a clean line in hindsight; it was not one while it was being walked.
>
> **Scale of the arc.** The work spans about **seven months**. The first three and a half, from the earliest design-direction prototypes at the end of February 2026, went through a formal scaffold that reached ~84 design-revision rounds and into a dedicated development repo where the two dead architectures below were built, measured and sealed. That repo accumulated a **ground-truth registry of 23 locked conclusions** (each admitted only after the user's own eyes verified the rendered pixels) plus 17 transparently marked conjectures, and **10+ preregistered experiments** (T1, T2, T3, T3b2/T3b3, T4, T5a/T5b) with bare controls, dual-primary metrics and third-party (Codex) audits. The clean release repo was split off in mid-June 2026. After a summer of production use, the kit was rebuilt on Claude Opus 5.5 in late September and early October 2026, through four more preregistered experiments. This repo's short git history reflects the release split, not the project's age.

## Four chapters, one goal

### 1. The Dogma Engine — *forbid the slop* (dead)

The first belief was the obvious one: if AI design is slop, **ban the slop**. The engine compiled a list of prohibitions — don't center everything, don't use the slop gradient, don't… — and **never looked at the rendered image** at all.

**Why it died:** across every model it converged to *the same ugliness*. Ruling out the bad is not the same as producing the good. The objective function had no "beauty" dimension in it, only "absence of named sins", and a piece can satisfy every prohibition and still be dead on arrival. Worse, a builder told to avoid the obvious will run *away* from it, and running away from the common answer is not the same as finding a better one. That lesson came back in chapter 4. (Registry: a bans-only engine is a negative-space attractor; it collapses across models to one ugliness.)

### 2. The MVC pipeline — *engineer the slop out* (dead)

The second belief was that the problem was *imprecision*: if you pinned design intent down rigorously enough and compiled it deterministically, quality would follow. The work split into Model → Controller → View. A "design brain" emitted **discrete intent** (a strict `SceneDraft`: narrative beats, per-beat composition, color and tempo intents, and a global aesthetic vector, **never** coordinates or numbers); a deterministic Controller compiled every continuous value (OKLCH colors, frame timelines, spring parameters, pixel boxes); the View rendered it verbatim; and a feedback loop judged the real frames (geometry, pixel CV, conceit).

It was real engineering, fully unit-tested and schema-validated. **Why it died:** two reasons. The deterministic-compile layer was enormously heavy. And splitting design from construction into isolated Model and View stages manufactured **translation loss**: the conversation went well, but intent was lost crossing the layer boundary ("HTML preview ≠ final render"). A single agent that *designs and builds in one continuous context* turned out to be simpler **and** stronger: it could reach in and fix the design at the execution layer, which the layered pipeline could not.

The MVC code is sealed and archived. The plugin carries **none** of it: no `SceneDraft`, no Controller, no deterministic compile, no MVC feedback-loop judges. (Carrying any of that under a 甲乙环 label would silently re-import the dead architecture's semantics, a thing we explicitly guard against.)

### 3. The 甲乙环 (critic loop) — *equip the designer, then judge the pixels* (the architecture that holds)

The third belief is the synthesis of the first two post-mortems: stop *forbidding* and stop *over-engineering*; **equip** the designer richly, let it design and build in one breath, and put a **design-blind** judge on the rendered pixels. It was born from a dialectic experiment (the first time the asymmetric 甲↔乙 arbitration was tested on real frames), then validated across a chain of experiments (T1, T2, T3, T3b2/T3b3, T4, T5a/T5b). **乙** designs and builds in one continuous context; **甲** is a design-blind aesthetic critic; the orchestrator ferries verdicts verbatim; the user's eyes are the final gate.

As first released in June 2026, the plugin wrapped that loop in a heavy kit, built for the models of the time: long design equipment with a strict three-step design process, N draws that each self-checked before a blind selector picked one, frame strips sampled at the pauses of the motion, and a closing "tempo pass" to repair timing. Each of those answered a real failure on those models. Chapter 4 is about what happened to them when the model changed.

### 4. The rebuild on Claude Opus 5.5 — *test every piece against the model* (current)

By late September 2026 the models had moved. With Claude Opus 5.5, front-end craft reached a new level, and the user's position was that equipment tuned against an older model's habits and limits could now be a ceiling for a stronger one. So every piece of the kit was put back on trial, against the current model, with preregistered experiments.

- **The equipment ablation** ([results](research/2026-09-25-equipment-ablation-results.md)). One draw each on three briefs, with full equipment, minimal equipment and none. The full equipment never ranked above the lighter arms on potential and came last on current finish on all three briefs. It was retired, and the minimal version became the new baseline. The same run surfaced a defect no frame-based check had seen: a line of type twitching in the final hold, which the builder's self-check and the selector both passed and the user saw in the video at once.
- **A wrong turn, caught before it ran.** The first follow-up draft set out to prompt the bare model toward "divergent" ideas. It was withdrawn the same day: it treated distance from the common answer as quality, the Dogma Engine's mistake in a new form. The rewrite diagnosed before it prescribed.
- **The idea diagnosis** ([results](research/2026-10-01-diagnosis-results.md)). Forty-five idea cards across three briefs, scored by the user. Independent runs converged hard (on one brief, five of six runs produced a single idea), the model's ranking of its own ideas was no better than chance, and visuals lifted a film at most one grade above its idea. So the ceiling sits upstream, in the idea. That nominated **dealing directions**: list N ideas that truly differ, give each draw one, always include the model's own first choice. The user's response reframed the pick as well: a judge's accuracy on paper means little when the choice depends on the visuals, and in production the human picking among previews is cheaper and faster, so the pipeline could default to the human pick.
- **The execution diagnosis** ([results](research/2026-10-01-execution-diagnosis-results.md)). Six films from strong ideas, no equipment, self-check only. Five were stunning, all six kept their core idea, and no common defect reached the threshold: good ideas plus the model's default execution were enough. It also showed, off the preregistration, how badly a frames-only reader handles time. It read designed transitions as defects and strip frame counts as durations, and a per-frame difference measured a slow twelve-second drift as no motion. That became the **time overview**.
- **Re-judging every prompt.** With that evidence, each line of the kit had to earn its place: a rule stays only if it states a clear viewer fact or a needed pipeline mechanism. The user ruled that equipment has no purpose unless there is a visible common defect for it to fix; the diagnosis had found none, so the design equipment was deleted in full. The tempo pass went too, because the time overview now shows every reviewer the time axis. The model then proposed deleting the critic loop as well, on two small pre-tests where the critic missed the user's own complaints. The user sent it back to the founding documents: the loop exists to catch defects that context corruption hides, not to match the user's taste, and in those same pre-tests it had caught real ones (white specks, jagged shadows) that the builder's self-check had passed. **The loop stayed**, as the default full-auto polish, and a second mode, *polish it yourself*, was added beside it.
- **Smaller rulings along the way.** A redraw may carry a comment, folded into the brief; the user may keep more than one draw at the pick; sound is on by default; defaults are not limits, so a user can ask for a TTS voice-over or bring their own assets.
- **The release acceptance** ([results](research/2026-10-02-release-acceptance-results.md)). The plugin against a blank control, same model and brief: plugin films 4/5 and 4/5 (one with no human feedback at all), the control 3/5. It passed, and it made the plugin's own hero film. It also surfaced two orchestrator lapses (no confirm-back of the commission; a pick-time comment ferried without showing the user the settled version first), which were fixed before release. And every sound in the plugin films had been synthesized by the builders themselves, well enough that the planned sound-effect pack was cut down to the recorded textures synthesis can't fake.
- **The Codex port** ([validation](CODEX-VALIDATION.md)). The same pipeline was generated as a second package, for Codex, from the same sources. Its acceptance took four end-to-end runs. Each void run surfaced a failure on the host side that the Claude runs never had:
  - the host's messages between agents turning into opaque tokens partway through a run;
  - an orchestrator announcing a finished film that the ledger didn't show;
  - builders registered but never spawned;
  - after a restart, the picked builder swapped for a fresh one.

  Each was answered in the Codex adapter and its launcher ledger, which now refuses whatever it can check. The shared sources the Claude plugin reads stayed as they were: a fix for one host's quirk would only be noise to the other. The fourth run went from the commission to a film the user accepted.

## Why the 甲乙环 is shaped the way it is

Every part of the current shape is a response to a specific failure the experiments surfaced.

- **乙 is one continuous context, not a layered handoff.** Because the MVC layer boundary lost intent in translation, the builder must be a *single* agent that holds the design and the code together, and it must be the *same* live instance from the first cut through every round of polishing (it has real memory of its own idea, trade-offs and code). A fresh agent reading back DESIGN.md and the code is only a *degraded rescue form*, never the product form. The one fresh-context step in the pipeline comes before any draw exists: the direction lister owns no idea and reads nothing back; it only seeds the builders.

- **Directions are dealt at the idea level.** Independent runs converge, so N undirected draws are mostly N copies of one idea. The lister names N ideas that differ in their core mechanism and key action; a shared subject, a shared palette or "both are common" doesn't make two ideas the same. Each builder gets one direction verbatim and never sees the others, and the lister's own first choice is always dealt. Nothing in the list asks for novelty.

- **The pick happens at the first cut, for potential.** Every draw stops at its first full render. The user watches those previews (video only, neutral labels, random order) and picks the one whose idea can go furthest, or hands the pick to a provenance-blind selector that does the same. A bold base with a few fixable nits beats a clean, safe, mediocre one, because polishing fixes nits but can't fix mediocrity. Only the picked draw self-checks. If none is right, the user redraws, optionally with a word about the batch that is folded into the brief; the new lister never sees the rejected directions, because "avoid what was tried" would turn the list into a novelty objective.

- **甲 is design-blind, persistent, and reports phenomena only.** Blind, because a model's read of an image bends toward any design context it holds (it defends the doc). Persistent, because a *fresh, memoryless* critic never converges: a brand-new eye each round keeps finding fresh nitpicks, over-fitting until the demands outrun the fixes. A critic that remembers its own prior calls holds a stable bar and lets the loop close. Phenomena only (severity, not prescriptions; no defect-type labels), because whether a phenomenon is an objective failure, an unrealized intent or a defensible choice is the *builder's* call; a critic that prescribes oversteps its blindness.

- **Every reviewer sees time, drawn from the pixels.** The critic is a vision model reading images, so what it is shown decides what it can judge. Uniform frame sampling over-weights bursts (a clean half-second move becomes a stack of mid-transition stills that reads as overlapping text) and hides long, smooth drifts. The first fix sampled the *punctuation* of motion, one held frame per pause plus mid frames tagged "read for motion only, never a defect". That made the critic fair about layout, but it discarded duration, so a separate tempo pass had to re-time the film after the loop. The time overview replaces both: thumbnails at a fixed interval aligned with motion curves, blank stretches, flashes, holds that never settle and short-lived text all on one time axis, plus a full-resolution settle frame at every moment the picture comes to rest, and the video itself to pull any frame from. The rule the punctuated sampler taught survives: a designed transition caught mid-motion is not a defect; judge what the picture settles into.

- **A duration the user named is a promise; a duration the model chose is not.** The line is drawn at the commission, not by reading intent afterwards. Whatever second-count the user picks or states is *locked*; accepting a proposed number **is** choosing it. Only the explicit option "don't constrain it, leave the length to the designer" makes the length *free*, and then the number the builder hard-wires is its own and can move. Under a lock, the builder fits the film inside the total. If the content genuinely doesn't fit, it reports `duration blocked` with the arithmetic (which beats don't fit, at what reading speed, how many seconds would solve it, what cutting would) and the user decides. Force-fitting the film to a number and silently overrunning one are both barred.

- **Two ways to polish.** The default is the critic loop: hands-off and safe; it catches what context corruption hides, and it doesn't break the ceiling on its own. The other is *polish it yourself*: no critic; the user watches, and whatever they say goes word for word to the same builder, which decides how to change the film and explains with pixel evidence anything it thinks shouldn't change. Anything the user says about a draw while picking it travels with the pick, under one fixed line, so the builder's self-check takes it in. The user is never asked what's missing or how to fix it.

- **The orchestrator ferries verbatim and never judges.** The critic must never see the design, so 甲 and 乙 never talk directly. A neutral relay passes only *phenomena* (critic to builder) and *pixel-grounded rebuttals* (builder to critic), and in *polish it yourself* the user's own words. It adds nothing of its own and never paraphrases a role's definition; every agent reads its own verbatim prompt. In the product the relay is **automatic**, not a human pasting text by hand.

- **The user's eyes are the final gate.** The selector and 甲 are vision-model reference judgments: useful, not ground truth. The human's eyes outrank every machine judge. This gate *accepts or rejects* the AI's output; it does not *author* it. The human is the verifier of last resort, not a designer in the loop, and autonomy of design and a human acceptance gate sit together without contradiction.

## Retired along the way

Each of these was right for the model it was written for, and each went when the evidence said it no longer helped.

- **The design equipment.** Typographic, color, composition and texture knowledge, a top-art-director framing and a falsifiable visual *conceit* ("the protagonist is a concrete thing or event, never a glow"). On older models it beat a bare brief, and it was the positive answer to the Dogma Engine's bans. On Claude Opus 5.5 it ranked below the lighter arms, and no common defect remained for it to fix. Archived in [`docs/archive/`](archive/).
- **The three-step design process.** Narrative, then texture, then engine plan, in that order as a hard rule, because a designer who knew the texture techniques while choosing the conceit drifted its protagonist from a thing or an event to a light effect. It went with the equipment.
- **The self-check persona rewrite.** On older models, a builder that designed with passion turned into a clerk ticking a KPI list the moment it entered self-check, writing "acceptable residue, ship it". The fix was the project's most-tuned text: put the designer who refuses to settle back into the self-check. The current builder gets one plain sentence instead: look at your real frames and time overview, and fix what you're not satisfied with until you are. On the current model, that was enough.
- **Self-check before the pick.** Older models' first cuts could be unusable, so every draw self-checked before anyone chose. On the current model the first cut is a fair baseline, so only the picked draw self-checks, and the losing draws cost one render each.
- **The punctuated frame strip and the tempo pass.** Replaced by the time overview, as described above.

## The experimental discipline that kept it honest

The architecture is trustworthy only because the process around it was disciplined.

- **A ground-truth whitelist.** In the development repo exactly one document was ground truth, the "phenomenon → conclusion" registry, and a finding was admitted only after the *user's eyes* confirmed it. Memory, preregistrations and session conclusions are hypotheses, not truth, until they clear that bar.
- **Preregistered judging criteria.** Each experiment fixed its endpoints and its judging criteria *before* the run, so the read-out could only reconcile against the preregistration, never move the goalposts to fit the result. The rebuild's diagnosis preregistrations were also audited by a second model (Codex) before they ran.
- **A bare control, every model generation.** Every piece of guidance is measured against the same model with less or none. What helps one generation can hold the next one back.
- **Blind where the judge is a model.** Selection and critique are blind to provenance and, for the critic, to the design: the same reason the architecture needs a blind critic. The user's own scoring needs no such blinding, because the user doesn't read the design docs; it's the model whose judgment a design doc sways.
- **The user's eyes outrank every vision model.** A machine `CONVERGED: YES` is a necessary signal, never a sufficient one; the human eye is the terminal gate.

## Where human judgment did the load-bearing work

Be exact about the register of this contribution, because it is easy to misread. It is **not** the human supplying design taste to the loop. The product's whole thesis is that the AI does the designing, and a human eye on the pixels would defeat the point. The human's load-bearing work was one level up, in *how the product was built*: the **experimental discipline, the directional insight, and the refusal to settle** that an AI does not supply for itself.

- **Insisting on first principles.** Refusing the cheap fix of `MUST`/`MANDATORY`-clamping the model into compliance, on the bet that a model which *understands the division of labor* will do the right thing for the right reason. Cutting the over-fitted guardrails (the discrete defect-label taxonomy, the "no-realism-drift" hard ban) that were just a checklist transcribed into the judging spine, the same disease as the slop they were meant to prevent. And, in the rebuild, holding the kit to one test: a rule stays only if it fixes a defect we can see. That test retired months of the project's own tuned work.
- **A hunch turned into the architecture's spine, as an instance of intuition rather than the product's "core".** Catching the directional-pollution gap (a model's read of an image bends toward the design doc it holds, so only a design-blind judge catches the real flaw) was a System-1 leap off a blurry phenomenon. It is worth noting precisely as an *example* of the kind of intuition the work runs on, not as the thing the product is "about". It happened to yield the blind critic and become a load-bearing design decision; what it really shows is the faculty that produced it.
- **Refuting the model's attractive but unsound conjectures.** A model under development offers a steady stream of plausible, ornamented hypotheses whose logic doesn't close. Each was sent back until it did: correcting a mislabeled defect (a CSS concentric-ring light wrongly called a "floating orb"); forcing a distinction the AI had blurred (a real 3D-lighting regression vs. a crude CSS simulation: *go read the source and prove which*); puncturing an overstated constraint (the claim that the builder *couldn't* be a continuous-context agent); catching "fixes" that were secretly regressions (a fix re-renders and re-scans the *whole* frame, because a repair in one place opens a tear exactly where the eye, fixed on the patched spot, won't look). In the rebuild: withdrawing the experiment that equated novelty with quality, and sending back the proposal to delete the critic loop.
- **Refusing the model's pessimism and its "downgrade" suggestions.** The most easily lost piece of the contribution, and one of the most decisive. Again and again the model would reason, from thin local evidence, toward a *lowered ceiling*: "this isn't reachable, ship the safer version." Holding the line for the top-tier target, on taste and conviction and with long-term patience, and refusing the downgrade until the pipeline actually reached the result, is what kept the project aimed at the tail instead of relaxing back to the mean.
- **Building a first-principles base so the direction never drifted.** `EMPIRICAL-FINDINGS` and the ground-truth registry exist so that months of work compound instead of wandering: every architectural turn forced by an observed phenomenon, admitted only after the user's own eyes confirmed it, never by a model's say-so.

What a token grant amplifies is precisely this: a method in which **one person's experimental discipline and refusal to settle drive an AI's execution bandwidth, design included,** to produce quality the AI doesn't reach alone. More compute is not more of the same. It is more directions and draws to widen the tail the pick chooses from, more polishing to carry a strong base further, more of the quality a single shot never reaches. Four chapters in, the goal is unchanged and now measured: a model that already designs well, pulled up to its own ceiling, while a human steers the *method*, not the pixels.
